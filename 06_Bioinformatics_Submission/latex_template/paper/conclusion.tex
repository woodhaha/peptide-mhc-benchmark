\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Architecture choice has a modest effect on peptide-MHC binding prediction: a CNN modestly outperformed a Deep FFN, the top four architectures fell within $\sim$2 percentage points, and a Random Forest was clearly worse. Random-label controls confirmed chance-level performance, ruling out noise overfitting. Canonical binding is largely solved by the anchor motif --- a zero-parameter anchor rule matches the trained model --- while non-canonical binders plateau at $\sim$0.65 sensitivity across three independent sequence-based predictors, defining the current frontier. Beyond peptide-MHC, the three artifacts we corrected --- data-size imbalance, a degenerate null, and stale checkpoints --- illustrate a general benchmark-composition failure mode that reproduces false conclusions across machine-learning benchmarks; the checklist in Box~1 is offered as a reusable safeguard. Experimental validation of the non-canonical tail is the decisive next step.

\begin{methods}
\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Training data and labels}
100,000 random 9-mer peptides were generated by uniform sampling of the 20 standard amino acids. Each was labelled with MHCflurry 2.2.0 against HLA-A*02:01 and assigned to Strong Binder (SB, percentile rank $<$0.5\%), Weak Binder (WB, 0.5\%$\leq$rank$<$2.0\%), or Non-Binder (NB, rank $\geq$2.0\%) following the netMHCpan convention \citep{Reynisson20}. The resulting distribution (1.7\% SB, 5.1\% WB, 93.2\% NB) was balanced by downsampling to 1,696 per class (n=5,088), partitioned 90/10 into training (n=4,579) and held-out test (n=509) sets with stratified sampling (seed 42). Two comparator labelling strategies were implemented: a PSSM encoding crystallographic A*02:01 anchor preferences, and random synthetic labels as a baseline control. An external benchmark of 49 experimentally validated IEDB epitopes plus 20 homopolymer negatives was curated \citep{Vita19}.

\subsection{Peptide encoding}
Each 9-mer was encoded with the BLOSUM62 matrix \citep{Henikoff92}, min--max normalized, producing a 9$\times$20 matrix flattened to 180 dimensions. ESM-2 embeddings \citep{Rives21} were extracted at t6 (2,880-dim per-position) and t12 (4,320-dim) depths; a mean-pooled t6 variant (320-dim) served as an ablation control destroying positional information.

\subsection{Model architectures}
Six classifiers were implemented: (i) FFN (180$\rightarrow$90$\rightarrow$3, 49K params); (ii) Deep FFN (180$\rightarrow$360$\rightarrow$180$\rightarrow$90$\rightarrow$45$\rightarrow$3 with batch normalisation and dropout, 152K params); (iii) CNN (Conv2D(32,3$\times$3) + FFN body, 1.05M params); (iv) LSTM(64)$\rightarrow$dense(32)$\rightarrow$3, 24K params; (v) ResNet-style stem + 3 residual blocks, 325K params; (vi) Random Forest (100 trees). All networks used cross-entropy loss with Adam or RMSprop (lr 0.001), 150 epochs, batch size 50, 20\% internal validation split, early stopping (patience 10), and learning-rate reduction on plateau. Five-fold stratified cross-validation was performed for the FFN. The held-out test set (509 peptides) was disjoint from training and used for all reported external metrics, satisfying the independent-test-set requirement.

\subsection{Protein scanning, mutation analysis, and structure}
Ten therapeutically relevant proteins were scanned with a sliding 9-mer window (stride 1; 3,536 windows). Sixteen recurrent hotspot mutations (7 p53, 9 KRAS) were analyzed by comparing wild-type and mutant binding scores; mutations were classified as Created/Enhanced/Destroyed/Unchanged. The KRAS G12V neoepitope YKLVVVGAV carries a non-canonical lysine at P2; we evaluated a predicted K-E63 salt bridge using comparative B-pocket geometry across eight HLA-A*02:01 crystal structures (PDB 1DUZ, 1AKJ, 1HHJ, 1JF1, 1QEW, 1QRN, 1S9W, 2GIT), energy minimisation (OpenMM, amber14 force field), and a Coulombic electrostatic estimate.

\subsection{Feature extraction}
Learned features were extracted from the penultimate dense layer via truncated models. Hierarchical clustering (Ward's method, Euclidean distance) was applied to representations of top epitope candidates; feature--binding-score correlation was assessed by Pearson's r with a 1,000-iteration permutation test.

\subsection{Multi-allele extension}
Experimentally confirmed class I binders were curated from IEDB for five common alleles (HLA-A*02:01, A*01:01, A*03:01, B*07:02, B*44:03), yielding n=14,488 positives (canonical 11,225; Type A 151; Type B 3,112). Type A (tolerated weak anchors) and Type B (energy-compensated non-canonical) were defined by whether the peptide violates the allele's canonical anchor motif at p2/p9. Protein-background negatives were sampled from natural proteomes. A rebalanced pan-specific DFFN (B*07:02 upsampled 4$\times$) was trained on pooled data with five seeds (0, 1, 42, 123, 2024). MHCflurry 2.2.1 (affinity $<$500\,nM) and MUNIS \citep{Chen25} (structure-based) served as independent comparators on the n=3,112 Type B population plus canonical controls. A 15-allele panel (pan16) tested scaling with per-allele minimum sample (2,000) upsampling. To address label circularity, temporal holdout compared IEDB epitopes published after 2022 against pre-2021 (in-distribution) epitopes at sensitivity threshold 0.95.

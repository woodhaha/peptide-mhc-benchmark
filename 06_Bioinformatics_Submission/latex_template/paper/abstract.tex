\abstract{\textbf{Motivation:} Peptide-MHC-I predictors are routinely trained on computationally labelled data, yet benchmark scores that merely reproduce the labeler's decision boundary are rarely distinguished from true binding accuracy. We name this failure mode \textbf{label circularity} and distil a benchmark-hygiene checklist.\
\textbf{Results:} Re-analysing a 5,190-peptide HLA-A*02:01 benchmark under controlled conditions dissolved three apparent findings --- per-allele superiority over a pan model, ``label quality dominates architecture'', and ``simple beats complex'' --- each traced to a distinct confound (a 10.6$\times$ data-size artifact, a degenerate null, and stale checkpoints). A zero-parameter anchor rule underperformed the trained model on non-canonical binders, which reached $\sim$0.65 sensitivity across four predictors at unmatched operating points, including BigMHC. A 6,524-affinity benchmark (AUC 0.942) and a post-2022 temporal holdout bound self-consistency and generalisation.\

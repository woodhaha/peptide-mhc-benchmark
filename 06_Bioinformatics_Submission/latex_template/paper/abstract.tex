\abstract{\textbf{Motivation:} Peptide-MHC-I binding-prediction benchmarks can manufacture false conclusions when confounders are uncontrolled.\
\textbf{Results:} We benchmarked six architectures on 5,190 MHCflurry-labelled HLA-A*02:01 9-mers with multi-seed estimates and re-analysed three apparent findings under controlled conditions. A convolutional network was modestly best (91.9\% $\pm$ 0.9\% vs.\ 90.6\% $\pm$ 1.2\% for a deep feed-forward network); random labels fell to chance ($\sim$33\%); and a claimed ``label quality dominates architecture'' result dissolved once the degenerate null control was removed. A zero-parameter anchor rule matched the trained model on canonical peptides, while non-canonical binders plateaued at $\sim$0.65 sensitivity across three independent predictors --- the true frontier. A temporal holdout of post-2022 epitopes preserved recognition, and a benchmark of 6,524 experimentally measured affinities (ROC AUC 0.942) confirmed the training labels' quality. We distil the artifacts into a benchmark-hygiene checklist applicable beyond peptide-MHC.\

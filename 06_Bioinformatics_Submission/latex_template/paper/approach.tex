\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Training data and architecture comparison}
MHCflurry labelling of 100,000 random 9-mers yielded 1.7\% SB, 5.1\% WB, 93.2\% NB; after balancing, 5,190 peptides (1,730/class). Across five training seeds, the CNN achieved the highest accuracy (91.9\% $\pm$ 0.9\%; Table~\ref{Tab:arch}, Figure~\ref{fig:arch}), modestly ahead of the Deep FFN (90.6\% $\pm$ 1.2\%), the FFN (89.8\% $\pm$ 1.1\%), and the LSTM (89.7\% $\pm$ 0.9\%) --- the top four architectures within $\sim$2 percentage points --- while ResNet (87.6\% $\pm$ 1.8\%) and Random Forest (79.7\% $\pm$ 0.4\%) were clearly worse. Two external predictors frame this comparison (Table~\ref{Tab:arch}): MHCflurry 2.2.1 itself scores 100\% on the labels it produced --- an expected ceiling, not evidence of superiority --- while the independent state-of-the-art predictor NetMHCpan-4.1, which was not trained on these labels, agrees with them on only 62.24\% (323/519). The 8.1-point gap between the labeler (100\%) and the best finite-sample approximation (91.9\% for the CNN) is information loss from finite samples; the far larger 28-point gap to NetMHCpan (62.24\%) is label circularity --- a model tracks the decision boundary of whichever labeler trained it (see Discussion). The WB class was consistently the most difficult (F1 0.752--0.880).

The modest spread among the top four architectures reflects the positional nature of peptide--MHC binding: anchor positions p2/p9 dominate, so architectures that preserve positional information perform comparably. ResNet and the Random Forest were clearly worse, consistent with the absence of translation invariance in a 9$\times$20 encoding and, for the Random Forest, the high dimensionality of the BLOSUM62 encoding.

\subsection{Label-source controls}
Label source, not architecture, sets the practical ceiling of what a model can learn (Table~\ref{Tab:label}, Figure~\ref{fig:label}). Training on MHCflurry labels reached 90.6\% accuracy, whereas a deterministic PSSM label source reached 82.6\% (on a separate 15,000-peptide pool, so not a clean head-to-head comparison) and an i.i.d.\ random-label null fell to chance (34.5 $\pm$ 1.7\%, close to the 33.3\% expected for three balanced classes). The random-label result confirms the models learn genuine signal rather than noise; the PSSM--MHCflurry gap, though confounded by pool size, suggests MHCflurry's labels are at least as learnable as a hand-crafted scoring matrix.

\subsection{Encoding}
On the FFN (the architecture used for all encoding comparisons), BLOSUM62 generalized stably under cross-validation (92.2 $\pm$ 0.4\%; per-fold 92.2/92.4/92.1/92.7/91.6\%). ESM-2 t6 per-position embeddings scored 93.3\% on a single split but collapsed to 82.2 $\pm$ 1.0\% under CV --- the 2,880-dimensional features overfit the 4,671-sample training set (Table~\ref{Tab:encoding}, Figure~\ref{fig:esm}). Mean-pooled t6 embeddings collapsed to 65.9\%, confirming that positional information is essential. The t12 variant (4,320-dim) overfit similarly (90.9--91.5\%). The single-split 93.3\% result reflects a favourable partition rather than a genuine encoding advantage at this training scale. ESM-2's representational advantage materialises only at large scale: the 650M checkpoint (11,520-dim per-peptide) reached 92.4 $\pm$ 0.5\% under CV, matching BLOSUM62 (92.2 $\pm$ 0.4\%) but at 64$\times$ the input dimensionality. At the data scale typical of single-allele studies, compact biologically motivated encodings such as BLOSUM62 therefore remain the more cost-effective choice, while the small ESM-2 checkpoints overfit.

\subsection{Held-out validation and translation}
The Deep FFN achieved 95.8\% sensitivity (46/48; Wilson 95\% CI 86--99\%), 75.0\% specificity (15/20), ROC AUC 0.953 against IEDB epitopes (Table~\ref{Tab:iedb}, Figure~\ref{fig:iedb}), after excluding ILRGSVAHK (influenza NP 265--273), which is HLA-B*08:01- rather than A*02:01-restricted. The two remaining false negatives (QYDPVAALF, TLGIVCPIC) carried non-canonical p9 anchors (F, C). An independent predictor, NetMHCpan-4.1 (queried through the IEDB web API, which does not pin a reproducible tool version), achieved 83.3\% sensitivity (40/48) on the same set. This gap partly reflects label circularity (44/49 of the epitopes overlap MHCflurry's training corpus), but NetMHCpan's pan-specific training on MS-elution-based labels makes this an indicative rather than clean comparison; the more direct circularity evidence is the 62.24\% label agreement (323/519 test peptides) reported in the Discussion. To contextualise the benchmark, we include a zero-parameter anchor-checker baseline (a rule classifying any 9-mer with canonical p2/p9 residues as a binder): it achieves sensitivity 1.0 on this canonical-motif-dominated set, matching the trained model (a comparison with only 3 discordant peptides, too low-powered for a formal test; the anchor rule has no tunable score threshold, so its precision---and hence F1---is fixed by the test-set composition rather than a free parameter) --- confirming that the canonical set overstates generalisation and that the informative signal lies in the non-canonical/hard-tail sensitivity. The five false positives were homopolymers ($\leq$2 unique amino acids) with canonical anchors --- biologically implausible binders whose low sequence complexity the classifier fails to penalise. A domain-knowledge filter ($\leq$2 unique amino acids $\rightarrow$ non-binder) removed all five, but we report 75.0\% as the primary, unfiltered specificity to avoid post-hoc inflation. To break this circularity, we additionally scored the Deep FFN against a large label-independent set of experimentally measured affinities --- 3,438 unique 9-mers with measured IC50 $<$500\,nM and 3,086 with IC50 $>$5,000\,nM (the 500--5,000\,nM grey zone was excluded to yield unambiguous binders and non-binders), restricted to direct binding assays (competitive fluorescence/radioactivity) and excluding MS-elution-derived, predictor-dependent entries. The MHCflurry-trained Deep FFN recognised 94.4\% of the experimental binders and rejected 74.5\% of the non-binders (threshold 0.5; ROC AUC 0.942); the rebalanced per-allele model reached 89.8\% sensitivity and 89.1\% specificity (AUC 0.945). This benchmark validates the quality of the training labels --- that MHCflurry's computational labels agree with experimentally measured binding --- rather than generalisation to unseen peptides, since the peptides are drawn from the same IEDB binding-affinity corpus on which the labeler was trained; generalisation to unseen peptides is instead established by the temporal holdout (Section 3.11). Protein scanning of 10 proteins (3,536 windows) recovered 10/11 known epitopes (91\%; Figure~\ref{fig:scan}), including immunodominant GILGFVFTL (M1), NLVPMVATV (pp65), and SLLMWITQC (NY-ESO-1); the single miss was the MART-1 clinical variant ELAGIGILTV, whose P2 leucine is a non-natural anchor-modified residue. Mutation scanning of 16 hotspot mutations identified 7 epitope-altering events (Table~\ref{Tab:mutation}, Figure~\ref{fig:mutation}). p53 R248W created the neoepitope MNWRPILTI, a mutation whose T-cell immunogenicity is documented in patients and in HLA-A2/DRB1-transgenic mice \citep{Malekzadeh20,Quandt18}; KRAS G12V created YKLVVVGAV via G$\rightarrow$V at p9 creating a canonical C-terminal anchor. Three mutations (KRAS G13D, KRAS A146T, p53 R249S) destroyed existing epitopes, representing potential immune-evasion mechanisms.

\subsection{A rebalanced pan model matches per-allele specialists on four of five alleles}
With protein-background negatives and a fixed threshold (n=25 non-canonical binders per allele, so these point estimates carry wide confidence intervals), per-allele models reached non-canonical sensitivity A*02:01 0.84, A*01:01 0.88, A*03:01 1.00, B*07:02 0.96, B*44:03 0.96 (Table~\ref{Tab:panvsperallele}; Figure~\ref{fig:pan}). The rebalanced pan model matched this on four of five alleles (A*02:01 0.84, A*01:01 0.88, A*03:01 1.00, B*44:03 1.00), but B*07:02 was the clear exception: the pan model dropped to 0.60 versus 0.96 per-allele (0.64 in the 15-allele pan16 panel; Wilson 95\% CIs [0.41, 0.77] vs [0.81, 0.99], non-overlapping), consistent with over-anchoring to B*07:02's distinctive P2-Proline --- a reproducible limitation rather than a fundamental pan-model deficiency. The earlier observation that per-allele models outperform pan models on non-canonical binders is not supported once per-allele training-data size is controlled.

\subsection{Canonical binders are recognized at $\sim$0.98 by every predictor}
On n = 11,225 canonical positives, all four predictors were saturated: DFFN 0.979, MHCflurry 0.986, MUNIS 0.950, BigMHC 0.950 (Table~\ref{Tab:predictor}). The canonical binder problem is effectively solved by all current methods.

\subsection{Non-canonical (Type B) binders plateau at $\sim$0.65 across predictor classes}
On n = 3,112 Type B positives, sensitivity was DFFN 0.607, MHCflurry 0.668, MUNIS 0.647, and BigMHC 0.660 (Table~\ref{Tab:predictor}; Figure~\ref{fig:plateau}). The four values fall within a narrow band (0.607--0.668), far below the $\sim$0.98 canonical sensitivity, and the attention-based BigMHC does not lift the ceiling either --- the plateau is shared across sequence architectures of varying complexity.

\subsection{Type A (tolerated weak anchors) is inherently hard}
On n = 151 Type A positives, DFFN achieved 0.477 and MHCflurry 0.669 (MUNIS was not evaluated on this small set). Retraining with broadened motifs did not recover Type A: A*03:01 was flat at 0.634, and B*07:02 Type B sensitivity dropped from 0.680 to 0.341 (Table~\ref{Tab:broadmotif}). The low Type A sensitivity is therefore not a training-data gap but an inherent property of these heterogeneous, weak, allele-specific binders (mass-spectrometry confirmed; 3--6\% cross-allele). The canonical-motif bias is what makes the model work.

\subsection{The extreme tail is prediction-resistant, cross-predictor}
The two false negatives from our single-allele IEDB benchmark (QYDPVAALF, TLGIVCPIC) and the KRAS G12V P2-Lys epitope YKLVVVGAV were scored as non-binders by all three predictors (MHCflurry 3.8--13.8 $\mu$M; DFFN 0.000--0.254; MUNIS 3/4 low), despite experimental evidence of peptide-induced HLA-A*02:01-restricted cytotoxic T-cell responses --- albeit the weakest of the three mutants screened in that study \citep{Wu20}. A single counterexample --- QYDPVAALF rescued by MUNIS (0.972 vs. sequence 0.000 / 6,199 nM) --- is encouraging but n = 1. Note that Table~\ref{Tab:mutation}'s ``CREATED'' label for KRAS G12V refers to the motif-level creation of a canonical P9 anchor (G$\rightarrow$V) relative to wild-type, not to a predicted binder: the mutant's absolute score remains below the binding threshold owing to the non-canonical P2-Lys.

\subsection{Scaling: one model approximates a 15-allele panel}
The rebalanced pan16 reached pooled non-canonical sensitivity 0.819 (307/375, @0.95), with 12/15 alleles at 0.76--1.0 (Table~\ref{Tab:pan16}; Figure~\ref{fig:pan16}). The weak tail --- B*27:05 (0.08), B*51:01 (0.52), B*07:02 (0.64), A*24:02 (0.76) --- reflects data scarcity and, for B*07:02, over-anchoring, not architecture.

\subsection{Temporal holdout breaks the label-circularity concern}
Post-2022 epitopes (unseen by the labeler) were recognized at sens@0.95: A*02:01 0.824, A*03:01 0.936, B*07:02 0.973 --- preserved versus pre-2021 (0.879/0.905/0.898), with no systematic drop in the post-2022 era (Table~\ref{Tab:temporal}; Figure~\ref{fig:temporal}). The model is not overfit to a single training-data era.

\subsection{Learned feature representations}
Feature extraction from the Deep FFN penultimate dense layer revealed biologically meaningful structure (Figure~\ref{fig:features}). t-SNE visualisation of its 45-dimensional penultimate representation showed partial class separation: SB peptides clustered distinctly (91\% of SB peptides fell within the SB cluster), while WB peptides formed a continuous transition zone between SB and NB clusters --- consistent with the graded affinity boundary in the 50--500 nM range. Integrated-gradients analysis identified position-specific anchor contributions: p9 dominated SB predictions, p2 and p3 contributed most to WB predictions, and NB predictions were driven by the absence of anchor-characteristic patterns. Hierarchical clustering separated validated epitopes into coherent clusters, with false negatives forming a distinct branch characterised by absent anchor-position activation. Five features showed Pearson $r > 0.7$ with binding score; by a 1,000-iteration permutation test, approximately 0.3 features would exceed this threshold by chance, suggesting these represent genuine binding-relevant representations rather than spurious correlations.

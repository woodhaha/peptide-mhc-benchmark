\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Training data and architecture comparison}
MHCflurry labelling of 100,000 random 9-mers yielded 1.7\% SB, 5.1\% WB, 93.2\% NB; after balancing, 5,190 peptides (1,730/class). Across five training seeds, the CNN achieved the highest accuracy (91.9\% $\pm$ 0.9\%; Table~\ref{Tab:arch}, Figure~\ref{fig:arch}), modestly ahead of the Deep FFN (90.6\% $\pm$ 1.2\%), the FFN (89.8\% $\pm$ 1.1\%), and the LSTM (89.7\% $\pm$ 0.9\%) --- the top four architectures within $\sim$2 percentage points --- while ResNet (87.6\% $\pm$ 1.8\%) and Random Forest (79.7\% $\pm$ 0.4\%) were clearly worse. MHCflurry itself scored 100\% on the test set it labelled, quantifying label circularity (see Discussion). The WB class was consistently the most difficult (F1 0.752--0.880).

The modest spread among the top four architectures reflects the positional nature of peptide--MHC binding: anchor positions p2/p9 dominate, so architectures that preserve positional information perform comparably. ResNet and the Random Forest were clearly worse, consistent with the absence of translation invariance in a 9$\times$20 encoding and, for the Random Forest, the high dimensionality of the BLOSUM62 encoding.

\subsection{Label-source controls}
Label source, not architecture, sets the practical ceiling of what a model can learn (Table~\ref{Tab:label}, Figure~\ref{fig:label}). Training on MHCflurry labels reached 90.6\% accuracy, whereas a deterministic PSSM label source reached 82.6\% (on a separate 15,000-peptide pool, so not a clean head-to-head comparison) and an i.i.d.\ random-label null fell to chance (34.5 $\pm$ 1.7\%, close to the 33.3\% expected for three balanced classes). The random-label result confirms the models learn genuine signal rather than noise; the PSSM--MHCflurry gap, though confounded by pool size, suggests MHCflurry's labels are at least as learnable as a hand-crafted scoring matrix.

\subsection{Encoding}
BLOSUM62 generalized stably under cross-validation (92.2 $\pm$ 0.4\%; per-fold 92.2/92.4/92.1/92.7/91.6\%). ESM-2 t6 per-position embeddings scored 93.3\% on a single split but collapsed to 82.2 $\pm$ 1.0\% under CV --- the 2,880-dimensional features overfit the 4,671-sample training set (Table~\ref{Tab:encoding}, Figure~\ref{fig:esm}). Mean-pooled t6 embeddings collapsed to 65.9\%, confirming that positional information is essential. The t12 variant (4,320-dim) overfit similarly (90.9--91.5\%). The single-split 93.3\% result reflects a favourable partition rather than a genuine encoding advantage at this training scale; at the data scale typical of single-allele studies, compact biologically motivated encodings such as BLOSUM62 remain the more robust choice, and ESM-2's representational advantage may require substantially larger training corpora to materialise.

\subsection{External validation and translation}
The Deep FFN achieved 95.8\% sensitivity (46/48), 75.0\% specificity (15/20), ROC AUC 0.953 against IEDB epitopes (Table~\ref{Tab:iedb}, Figure~\ref{fig:iedb}), after excluding ILRGSVAHK (influenza NP 265--273), which is HLA-B*08:01- rather than A*02:01-restricted. The two remaining false negatives (QYDPVAALF, TLGIVCPIC) carried non-canonical p9 anchors (F, C). An independent predictor, NetMHCpan-4.1, achieved 83.3\% sensitivity (40/48) on the same set. This gap partly reflects label circularity (44/49 of the epitopes overlap MHCflurry's training corpus), but NetMHCpan's pan-specific training on MS-elution-based labels makes this an indicative rather than clean comparison; the more direct circularity evidence is the 62.24\% label agreement reported in the Discussion. To contextualise the benchmark, we include a zero-parameter anchor-checker baseline (a rule classifying any 9-mer with canonical p2/p9 residues as a binder): it achieves sensitivity 1.0 and F1 0.85--1.0 on this canonical-motif-dominated set, matching the trained model --- confirming that the canonical set overstates generalisation and that the informative signal lies in the non-canonical/hard-tail sensitivity. The five false positives were homopolymers ($\leq$2 unique amino acids) with canonical anchors --- biologically implausible binders whose low sequence complexity the classifier fails to penalise. A domain-knowledge filter ($\leq$2 unique amino acids $\rightarrow$ non-binder) removed all five, but we report 75.0\% as the primary, unfiltered specificity to avoid post-hoc inflation. Protein scanning of 10 proteins (3,536 windows) recovered 10/11 known epitopes (91\%; Figure~\ref{fig:scan}), including immunodominant GILGFVFTL (M1), NLVPMVATV (pp65), and SLLMWITQC (NY-ESO-1). Mutation scanning of 16 hotspot mutations identified 7 epitope-altering events (Table~\ref{Tab:mutation}, Figure~\ref{fig:mutation}). p53 R248W created the neoepitope MNWRPILTI; KRAS G12V created YKLVVVGAV via G$\rightarrow$V at p9 creating a canonical C-terminal anchor. Three mutations (KRAS G13D, KRAS A146T, p53 R249S) destroyed existing epitopes, representing potential immune-evasion mechanisms.

\subsection{Structural hypothesis}
Template-based analysis of eight HLA-A*02:01 crystal structures (Figure~\ref{fig:structure}) showed a conserved B-pocket with Glu63 positioned 4.7 $\pm$ 0.2 \AA{} from the P2 C$\alpha$. Energy minimisation refined the P2 C$\alpha$--Glu63 OE2 distance to 3.7 \AA{}, within the reach of a lysine side chain; note that a proper salt-bridge geometry requires the side-chain N$\zeta$--OE2 distance rather than the backbone C$\alpha$ --- which we did not compute here, so the C$\alpha$-based distances are a coarse proxy only --- and an un-solvated Coulombic estimate would overstate the stabilisation (a buried salt bridge typically contributes only a few kcal/mol after desolvation). We therefore present only qualitatively the hypothesis that a charged P2 lysine could achieve stable binding through electrostatic compensation, pending a solvated free-energy calculation and experimental validation (e.g., X-ray crystallography or cryo-EM of the YKLVVVGAV--HLA-A*02:01 complex). In contrast, the p53 R248W neoepitope MNWRPILTI carries asparagine at P2, and its improved predicted binding relative to wild-type (MNRRPILTI) is attributable to the P3 Arg$\rightarrow$Trp substitution enhancing hydrophobic packing rather than any P2-mediated mechanism.

\subsection{Multi-allele generalization}
With protein-background negatives, per-allele models reached non-canonical sensitivity 0.84--0.96, and a rebalanced pan model matched them on four of five alleles (A*02:01 0.84, A*01:01 0.88, A*03:01 1.00, B*44:03 1.00); B*07:02 was a notable exception (pan 0.60 vs per-allele 0.96; 0.64 in the 15-allele pan16 panel, Table~\ref{Tab:pan16}), consistent with over-anchoring to P2-Proline (Figure~\ref{fig:pan}). This corrects an earlier observation that per-allele models outperform pan models on non-canonical binders: that apparent advantage was a 10.6$\times$ training-data-size artifact, not biology. On the 15-allele panel, pooled non-canonical sensitivity was 0.819 (307/375), with 12/15 alleles at 0.76--1.0. The weak tail (B*27:05, B*51:01, B*07:02, A*24:02) reflected data scarcity and, for B*07:02, over-anchoring, not architecture. Temporal holdout (three alleles with sufficient post-2022 data) confirmed preserved recognition of epitopes published after the labeler's training cutoff at sens@0.95: A*02:01 0.824, A*03:01 0.936, B*07:02 0.973 (versus pre-2021 0.879/0.905/0.898), indicating the model is not overfit to a single training-data era.

\subsection{Non-canonical plateau}
Canonical binders were recognized at $\sim$0.98 by every predictor class: DFFN 0.979, MHCflurry 0.986, MUNIS 0.950. Non-canonical (Type B) binders plateaued at $\sim$0.65: DFFN 0.607, MHCflurry 0.668, MUNIS 0.647 (Table~\ref{Tab:plateau}) --- a second, ESM-2-based sequence model did not lift the ceiling. Type A (tolerated weak anchors) binders were inherently hard (DFFN 0.477, MHCflurry 0.669) and not recovered by broad-motif retraining (A*03:01 flat at 0.634; B*07:02 Type B dropped 0.680$\rightarrow$0.341). The extreme energy-compensated tail (e.g., KRAS G12V P2-Lys; P2-Lys/Arg/His sidechains, maximum extension 7.6/7.3/4.4 \AA{}, span the 3.83 \AA{} P2--Glu63 gap) was scored as non-binder by all three predictors. Note that Table~\ref{Tab:mutation}'s ``CREATED'' label for KRAS G12V refers to the motif-level creation of a canonical P9 anchor (G$\rightarrow$V) relative to wild-type, not to a predicted binder: the mutant's absolute score remains below the binding threshold owing to the non-canonical P2-Lys.

\subsection{Learned feature representations}
Feature extraction from the penultimate dense layer revealed biologically meaningful structure (Figure~\ref{fig:features}). t-SNE visualisation of the 45-dimensional penultimate layer showed partial class separation: SB peptides clustered distinctly (correct classification rate 91\%), while WB peptides formed a continuous transition zone between SB and NB clusters --- consistent with the graded affinity boundary in the 50--500 nM range. Integrated-gradients analysis identified position-specific anchor contributions: p9 dominated SB predictions, p2 and p3 contributed most to WB predictions, and NB predictions were driven by the absence of anchor-characteristic patterns. Hierarchical clustering separated validated epitopes into coherent clusters, with false negatives forming a distinct branch characterised by absent anchor-position activation. Five features showed Pearson $r > 0.7$ with binding score; by a 1,000-iteration permutation test, approximately 0.3 features would exceed this threshold by chance, suggesting these represent genuine binding-relevant representations rather than spurious correlations.
